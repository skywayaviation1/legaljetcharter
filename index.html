Why the certificate matters

  1. Illegal charter is a flight sold without the right certificate or without the certificate holder in control. The FAA’s Safe Air Charter campaign is the public warning for that.
  2. The name on the brochure may be a marketing name. The certificate holder — the company with operational control — is the name that has to be on the FAA list.
  3. The airplane has to be authorized too. A Part 135 holder’s tail numbers are in the FAA workbook. A different tail is a different question.

If an operator or tail number is not in the FAA file, the FAA says to contact the local Flight Standards District Office rather than assume either way. Being listed is not a safety rating, an insurance check, or a promise the airplane is free.

What you are checking

What is Part 135?

14 CFR Part 135 is the FAA rule for commuter and on-demand operations. That is the certificate most U.S. air-taxi and charter operators need when they fly people for hire. Part 121 is the rule for scheduled airlines. A company can hold more than one kind of authority; the FAA workbook marks some aircraft as 121/135.

Source: 14 CFR Part 135 and the FAA’s Part 135 certification page.

How do I verify an operator?

Use the legal name from the quote, not only the brand. Search it here, then check the certificate designator and the N-number of the airplane that will actually fly. The airplane should be on that holder’s list in the FAA workbook.

The FAA’s own instructions: use the Part 135 operators and aircraft spreadsheet to see whether an operator is certificated for charter and which aircraft are authorized, and use Air Operator search for the holder or DBA. If you do not find the operator or the airplane, contact the local FSDO. This site is a copy of those two files, refreshed when the FAA changes them. The FAA pages remain the source of record.

What should I ask a broker?
  • Who is the direct air carrier — the legal name of the Part 135 certificate holder — and who has operational control of the flight?
  • What is the certificate number and the designator code?
  • What is the N-number of the aircraft that will fly, and is that tail authorized for that certificate?
  • If the airplane changes, ask for the new tail and check that one too.

A broker arranging a trip is not the same thing as the certificate holder operating it.

Why would an operator be on only one FAA list?

The workbook is an aircraft-level list of Part 135 holders. Air Operator search is a separate FAA directory of certificate holders, DBAs, and locations, filtered here to Part 135. They are updated on their own schedules. A holder can appear in one file before the other. Those rows are kept and labeled. They are not deleted to make the lists agree, and missing fields are left blank rather than guessed.

What does a listing not prove?

A row means the FAA file showed that holder, and for the workbook, those aircraft, as of the file date on this page. It does not mean the airplane is available, insured for your trip, or acceptable to a travel policy. The separate watchlist notes on a few operators are editorial. They are not FAA findings.
